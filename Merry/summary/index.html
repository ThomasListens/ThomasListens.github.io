01

sEEG Sonification Toolkit · v8.3, 2026

A browser-based synthesizer that translates EEG into navigable sound. No dependencies; runs on standard hardware.

  • Input: native EDF/EDF+ parser with automatic channel classification (sEEG shafts, scalp 10-20/10-10, auxiliary/physiological).
  • Preprocessing: DC removal, 50/60 Hz notch filtering, bipolar or common-average re-referencing.
  • Feature extraction: 21 features per channel — multi-scale RMS, Hilbert envelope, band power (δ–high γ) with automatic dominant-band weighting, spectral centroid, Hjorth mobility and complexity, permutation entropy, line length, spectral flux, and spike detection.
  • Synthesis: features drive an additive/FM engine, so changes in brain state are heard as changes in loudness, timbre, and transients.
  • Export: audio, automation envelopes (CSV), and wavetables.
  • Demo data: HUP intracranial EEG dataset (epilepsy monitoring).
  • Validation: independently reviewed by Teunis van Beelen, creator of EDFbrowser — “impressive and very interesting.”
  • Live: thomaslistens.github.io/seeg-sonification
  • Code: github.com/ThomasListens/ThomasListens.github.io/tree/main/seeg-sonification
02

Relative Tempo Derivation (RTD) · 2026

Provisional patent filed.

RTD is a method for estimating a person’s temporal state from the ratios between their physiological rhythms (heart rate, cadence, alpha frequency), each normalised to a personal baseline, and expressing it as a single tempo.

Timing is everything, and everyone moves to their own beat. The body keeps many clocks; RTD measures three of them, physiological and EEG together, and composes one tempo:

Tempo = HR × m × μ

  • HR: the pulse, the base clock.
  • m: how step rate couples to heart rate, bounded by just-intonation intervals: 4/3 at rest, 3/2 in sync, toward 3/1 at a run.
  • μ: the EEG term. Individual alpha frequency against the person’s own baseline.

The tempo is one output. Underneath it is a way to read a person’s state from a wearable:

  • Personal baseline: every signal is compared with the wearer’s own baseline, not a population norm, so a change means a change in that person.
  • Several signals, one reading: pulse, HRV, motion and EEG are read together. When a live signal drops out, the estimate falls back to a prior instead of failing.
  • Cold start: before any sensor data exists, each value starts from an estimate built on age, sex, height and weight. Measured signal replaces it within minutes.

Demonstrated on the MoBI dataset (IEEE DataPort): synchronized EEG, IMU, and pulse recorded during walking.

  • Pipeline: ASR artifact cleaning and ICA (64 channels, 22 components rejected); HR and HRV (RMSSD) from pulse; cadence from waist IMU; per-band center-of-gravity frequencies, z-scored against session baseline.
  • State detection: identified a multimodal state change at approximately minute 20 — HR drop, HRV rise, alpha slowing to −2σ, theta dip — that no single signal showed clearly on its own.
  • Output: runs end-to-end with a live signal dashboard and real-time tempo output.
03

Auditory Nerve Phase-Locking Analysis · 2025

Analyzed single-fiber auditory nerve recordings from cats (Peterson & Heil, 2020): responses to calibrated 1.2–1.3 kHz tones across 17 sound levels.

  • Built Python visualizations of phase locking and synaptic delay.
  • Built a sonification rendering neural spikes as audio aligned to the stimulus cycle.

This work examined how the auditory system encodes sound across intensity levels, and was the starting point for the sonification work above.

04

Close Your Eyes (一片心安) — EEG-Driven Composition and Film · released Sept 2026

Not just a lullaby, but a composition: the music and the visuals both integrate sleep EEG directly from my synthesizer. Deep-sleep recordings from OpenNeuro (ds004902, ds004551) run through the sEEG engine, where delta-band dynamics automate the just-intonation harmony and the same signal moves the image.

Completed and commercially released in English and Mandarin — EEG taken from a technical pipeline to a finished product.

Eye: Human Organ Atlas, LADAF-2024-38, imaged at the ESRF. CC BY 4.0. DOI 10.15151/ESRF-DC-2378105388 and 10.15151/ESRF-DC-2378105372.
